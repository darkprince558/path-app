# Recovering walls and floors from a hallway turn

Anish tested the first two methods on Apple Silicon using the existing undistorted frames, camera poses and fused point cloud. A camera that turns in place sees new directions but provides little triangulation baseline. Walking more slowly can reduce blur. A second viewpoint displaced sideways supplies new depth evidence.

## Methods and libraries

1. **Check the per-camera OpenMVS depth maps.** The plane pipeline produces a separate set of maps using `--remove-dmaps 0` and the eligible training photos. It checks seven interior samples per triangle, ignoring missing depths and neighborhoods with a depth discontinuity. A patch is rejected when at least five samples are clearly in front of measured depth in two sufficiently separated cameras, including when another camera supports it. On this Mac, 98 training photos yielded 89 maps. The baseline camera calibration and sparse landmarks use all 126 views. [OpenMVS options](https://github.com/cdcseacave/openMVS/blob/v2.4.0/apps/DensifyPointCloud/DensifyPointCloud.cpp) · [Schönberger et al., pixelwise view selection and geometric consistency](https://demuc.de/papers/schoenberger2016mvs.pdf)
2. **Compare the fitter with Open3D planar patches.** Estimate normals on a downsampled copy of the same cloud. Open3D splits the points into local neighborhoods, tests their planarity, then grows and merges patches. Its output boxes enclose the detections. Clip footprints to connected inliers and reapply the same opening and visibility checks to each proposal. This compares two plane detectors while holding capture, texture and display fixed. MIT code, no weights, and an official Python 3.12 wheel for Apple Silicon. [Araújo & Oliveira, robust planar patch detection](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2019.107115) · [Open3D tutorial](https://www.open3d.org/docs/release/tutorial/geometry/pointcloud.html#Planar-patch-detection) · [license](https://github.com/isl-org/Open3D/blob/main/LICENSE) · [wheel files](https://pypi.org/project/open3d/0.19.0/#files)
3. **Test LIMAP where line tracks persist.** Use the known cameras and its classical line detector/matcher to triangulate wall edges and doorframes. Test whether line reprojection and optional joint point/line bundle adjustment improve poses and boundary alignment. A line alone cannot establish a face or distinguish a painted seam from an opening. The BSD-3-Clause code documents macOS compilation with OpenMP. The classical path does not require learned weights. [Liu et al., 3D Line Mapping Revisited](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Liu_3D_Line_Mapping_Revisited_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf) · [implementation](https://github.com/cvg/limap) · [macOS dependencies](https://github.com/cvg/limap/blob/main/misc/install/dependencies.md) · [license](https://github.com/cvg/limap/blob/main/LICENSE)
4. **Benchmark ACMMP on NVIDIA hardware.** Its multiscale PatchMatch incorporates plane priors *during depth estimation*, a chance to recover weakly textured wall pixels before fusion. Convert the existing COLMAP cameras with the authors' converter. The reference implementation uses CUDA and carries an MIT license, with no learned weights. Score any improvements against doorway occlusion. [Xu et al., multiscale geometric consistency](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2022.3200074) · [official implementation and input converter](https://github.com/GhiXu/ACMMP) · [license](https://github.com/GhiXu/ACMMP/blob/main/LICENSE)
5. **Treat SimpleRecon as a research benchmark.** Its pretrained indoor depth model uses posed RGB images. The official COLMAP adapter asks for metric-scale camera poses and field-of-view adjustment. A measured length in the scene could set the scale for this recording. The authors tested Linux/CUDA. The release restricts commercial use, including use of resulting weights in commercial products. Check the license against the intended benchmark before using a checkpoint. [Sayed et al., SimpleRecon](https://nianticlabs.github.io/simplerecon/resources/SimpleRecon.pdf) · [COLMAP adapter and models](https://github.com/nianticlabs/simplerecon#-colmap-dataset) · [license](https://github.com/nianticlabs/simplerecon/blob/main/LICENSE)

## What the same video supports

At 5.0 s, 1,316 of 1,530 tracked landmarks had more than 5° of viewing-angle change. At 8.375 s, none of the 147 tracked landmarks did. Their median angle was 1.4°. Across the requested turn, the image-based depth sweep recovered 2,035 corroborated anchor locations, all between 6.25 and 7.875 s. Reading the native depth maps before fusion added 2,506 corroborated locations beyond the cleaned cloud. In seven reserved turn views the resulting mesh fills 5,666 previously empty pixels, compared with 2,587 in the earlier sweep-only trial. The adopted configuration also applied a stricter final depth veto. The distribution helps explain why a global plane fit alone adds little at the worst view. [Measured export and coverage](mac-video-trial.md#plane-fills-at-the-turn)

A native OpenCV test used 33 camera triples centered between 5.0 and 9.0 s, at the 450 × 800 working resolution. It detected segments at least 40 pixels long, matched samples by forward/backward optical flow, triangulated the neighboring lines with a minimum 3° angle and checked both center-frame endpoints within 2.5 pixels. At least one line passed in 12/33 triples with neighbors 0.125 s away and in 3/33 with neighbors 0.250 s away. At 5.500, 5.625 and 5.750 s, 49, 77 and 93 long segments were visible respectively. None survived three-view matching at either spacing. This measures one classical matching configuration. LIMAP, ACMMP and SimpleRecon remain untested on this video. The script and per-camera results are in the local run's ignored `line-screen/` folder.

Open3D 0.19.0 also ran on Apple Silicon CPU on the 480,718-point cleaned viewer cloud. With the same detector settings and two voxel sizes, it proposed 22 boxes from 51,201 samples and 35 from 95,239. In the finer pass, 20 were within 17° of vertical and 12 within 22° of horizontal, relative to the viewer axis. Horizontal patches can include ceilings and furniture. Downsampling, normal estimation and detection together took 0.35 s on the finer pass, excluding import and I/O. These boxes still need footprint, opening and view checks before contributing geometry. Settings, boxes and script are in the local run’s ignored `open3d-screen/` folder.

Report surface coverage gains in percentage points separately for wall and floor, doorway pixels newly blocked by the patch, and the fraction of valid depth comparisons contradicted. Keep cameras, crop, resolution and texture fixed. Show added triangles in a distinct color for inspection. Any credible blockage of a known opening rules out that patch. Existing camera estimation and densification used the full sequence. An independent end-to-end test requires withholding frames before densification and localizing them afterward.
